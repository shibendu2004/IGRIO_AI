Crop & Soil Reference Guide

Built on FAO-56 crop coefficients (Kc), root depths, and soil available water capacity (AWC)—with a clear path to future machine-learning models once farm history grows.

🌾

Rice (Paddy)

Cereal • Root Depth ~0.50 m

Kc: Initial 1.00 • Mid 1.20 • Late 0.90. High water sensitivity during active tillering and flowering.

🌿

Wheat

Cereal • Root Depth ~0.90 m

Kc: Initial 0.40 • Mid 1.15 • Late 0.40. Responds strongly to timely watering at crown-root and grain-fill stages.

🌽

Maize (Corn)

Cereal • Root Depth ~1.00 m

Kc: Initial 0.35 • Mid 1.20 • Late 0.60. Deep roots buffer short dry spells, but silking stage needs steady moisture.

🍅

Tomato

Horticulture • Root Depth ~0.60 m

Kc: Initial 0.60 • Mid 1.15 • Late 0.80. Frequent, measured watering supports flowering, fruit set, and even fruit size.

🥔

Potato

Tuber Crop • Root Depth ~0.60 m

Kc: Initial 0.50 • Mid 1.15 • Late 0.75. Consistent moisture during tuber initiation and bulking helps protect yield and quality.

🌱 Soil Water-Holding Categories & Zero-Hardware Design

IGRIO supports Sandy, Loamy, Clay, and Alluvial soils with distinct field capacity, wilting point, and available water capacity parameters.

Software-Only Architecture: Version 1 requires zero physical soil-moisture sensors, IoT boards, or automated pump controllers. All soil moisture figures are transparent software estimates.

📈 Transparent FAO-56 Baseline & Future ML Roadmap

Recommendations currently use transparent, scientifically grounded FAO-56 evapotranspiration and root-zone water-balance calculations—not an unvalidated black-box model.

Once sufficient historical farm logs and weather records are collected, Python regression models (such as Random Forest or Gradient Boosting) can be trained and validated to refine field-specific schedules.

Field Reference for Farmers

Know Your Soil Before You Irrigate

Choose the soil type that most closely matches your field. It changes how quickly water enters, drains, and remains available to crop roots.

🏖️

Sandy Soil

Low water holding • Drains very quickly

Water in smaller amounts more often. Check moisture sooner after hot or windy days; large applications can move below the root zone.

🌱

Loamy Soil

Balanced water holding • Good drainage

Usually the most forgiving soil. Follow the recommended volume, then adjust only after checking rainfall, field wetness, and crop stage.

🧱

Clay Soil

High water holding • Slow drainage

Irrigate more slowly and allow time for water to soak in. Avoid watering again when the surface is already wet or puddles remain.

🏞️

Alluvial Soil

Fertile, layered soil • Moderate to high water holding

Check different parts of the plot, because river-basin soils can vary. Use measured applications and account for recent rain before pumping.

✅ When Irrigation Is Usually Helpful

  • Water early morning or near sunset to reduce evaporation and wind drift.
  • Prioritise seedling establishment, flowering, fruit set, tuber bulking, and grain filling when the crop is sensitive to water stress.
  • Use the IGRIO recommendation after checking field moisture by hand and reviewing the rainfall forecast.
  • Apply water slowly enough for it to enter the root zone without running off the field.

⚠️ When to Delay or Avoid Irrigation

  • Delay if meaningful rain is expected soon or rain has already left the soil wet.
  • Do not irrigate waterlogged fields, standing-water areas, or plots with runoff; excess water can reduce root oxygen and nutrient uptake.
  • Avoid midday watering in hot, windy conditions unless it is necessary to prevent severe crop stress.
  • Pause and inspect leaks, broken sprinklers, or blocked drippers before running the system again.
